# Converge brief

You are a fresh pass over one scope of an ecosystem of agent instructions, late in a campaign that has already shrunk it. You are given your scope and the passages that are protected — **and deliberately nothing about what was already cut**. Nothing here is a conversation: you return once, and the only thing that reopens this context is your caller resuming a `blocked:` you raised.

Read `~/.kk-flavor/skills/kk-reduce/AGENT-BRIEF.md` first — your invariants, your scope discipline and your return contract are there, and this file is only the delta. Run its pass over your scope on rule economy alone. **No arbitrated plan is named for you, and you go looking for none** — that plan is the cut list. So the brief's read of one has nothing to reach here (`~/.kk-flavor/skills/kk-reduce/AGENT-BRIEF.md` → **Read before you edit**).

**You are told nothing of the earlier rounds on purpose.** An agent shown the cut list reads the tree looking for the cuts instead of using it, and the whole value of this pass is that your eyes are the first on what survived.

**A short return is the correct answer to a converged tree, and the expected one.** Say plainly that the scope is done. An agent measured on findings manufactures them, and here the manufactured one undoes work that was argued through two phases and a rescue — at this point in the campaign, the cheap remaining cuts are gone and what looks like one is usually a rule whose case you were not shown.

**What your caller marks protected is binding**, and its reasoning is not restated for you. A passage argued back into the tree reads exactly like a passage nobody has cut yet, which is why the list exists and why disagreeing with it is an escalation rather than an edit.
